Selling an inherited house as-is: what buyers actually expect

Most probate homes in Orange County sell as-is, and that is fine. What matters is presenting the house honestly and pricing it right.

By Vennessa Mele, DRE #02115354 · Orange County, California · Last reviewed September 2026

Short answer: you do not need to renovate. Most probate buyers expect a dated house and price for it. What you should do is clear it out, keep it safe, disclose what you know, and price it to the market rather than to a wish.

What "as-is" means in a probate sale

As-is means the estate will not make repairs or give credits for condition. It does not mean no disclosure. Because you did not live in the house, you are exempt from the usual Transfer Disclosure Statement, but you must still disclose anything you actually know: a roof leak, a foundation report, an unpermitted addition. Buyers can and will inspect.

Worth doing

  • Remove personal belongings, or at least clear pathways and surfaces. Buyers cannot see a house through fifty years of things.
  • Fix anything that is a safety issue: a broken step, exposed wiring, a leaking water heater.
  • Keep the yard mowed and the exterior tidy. First impressions still decide who walks in.
  • Have the utilities on for showings and inspections.
  • Consider a pre-listing home inspection. Knowing the roof is at end of life lets you price for it instead of renegotiating later.

Usually not worth doing

  • Kitchen and bath remodels. Buyers of dated Orange County homes generally plan their own.
  • New flooring or paint throughout, unless the house is in truly rough shape. Clean and empty usually reads better than a rushed flip.
  • Anything the estate has to borrow money to do.

Who buys probate homes

Three kinds of buyers: families who want the neighborhood and will renovate over time, small builders and flippers, and investors. The first group pays the most and is the group a well-marketed listing reaches. Off-market cash offers almost always come from the third group, at a discount of 15 to 30 percent. More on that here.
